Jim's reaction to existing indices:
---------------------------------------------------------------
1) It is indeed true that the current indices are empirical premises (national autonomy, workability, democracy and politicization are all of the form if X occurs Y will likely result). Tradition index is more basic values).

2) So much of the deliberation was about empirical premises that were very contested so I could see an argument for including them. In other words much of the deliberation was about whether a directly elected president would become more powerful than the current governor general, or not…this is a contested empirical premise. But it is not the same as what we usually call a policy attitude… which takes the form x should be done….
------------------------------------------------

Suggestions:
--------------------------------------------------------
1) A14 and A15 are certainly policy prescriptions. Whether there should be  a directly elected president or a president chosen by parliament or whether Australia should retain the queen… However, they are in the form of ranking choices, first choice, second choice etc.…A 15 is a prescription about whether or not the proposed president should be more or less powerful or the same as the current governor general in the amount of power… so this is also a policy attitude. Not sure how we use the ranking question as an index…it is really three questions….

Here is an idea for what to do with the all important ranking question:
 
A14p1 and A14p2
 
We make three indices: one is about degree of support for directly elected president, one is about degree of support for a president chosen by parliament and one is about retaining the queen.
For each option we have information about whether it is ranked first (from a14p1) or ranked second (from a14p2) or ranked third of the three (if it is neither of the first two). We assign a numerical value for degree of support for it being a first a second or third option of the three…. So we have three indices. What do you think?

2)      C4, C5p1, C5p2 and  C5p3 are also policy attitudes—whether or not Australia should change its flag, or whether the head of state should be a politician, or should be an Australian or whether Australia should cut its remaining constitutional ties to Britain…. The should or should not form make them policy attitudes…

3)      A8 is also a policy attitude… 
do you think that voting in constitutional referendums should be compulsory?
