This is the codebook for the National Issues Convention.  This codebook 
contains variables for Time 1, Time 2, and Time 3.

The NIC sample was drawn, interviewed, and recruited to Austin by NORC.  
The initial survey went into the field November 4, 1995 and ended 
January 18, 1996.  At time 1, 911 respondents were successfully interviewed.  
This codebook includes data for 638 Time 2 respondents.  466 of these Time 2 
respondents actually showed up at the DOP and 172 of the Time 2 respondents 
did not attend the DOP meeting but were reinterviewed by telephone at 
approximately the same time (466+172=638). For the follow-up interview 
(Time 3), there were 387 respondents.

The NIC had two quasi-control groups.  The first consisted of a random 
subsample of the initial interviewees who did not attend the deliberations.  
These individuals were re-interviewed by telephone at roughly the same time 
as the deliberations were ending.  NORC randomly selected these 
non-participants and interviewed 172 of them.  These 172 respondents are 
included in the Time 2 data that corresponds with this codebook.  The second 
quasi-control group consisted of an independent national random digit dial 
(RDD) sample interviewed by telephone, also at roughly the same time as the 
deliberations were ending.  There were 431 respondents.  The codebook for 
the second quasi-control group is called nicrdd.code and can be found at:
     /home/gov/staff/poll96/nic/codebooks/nicrdd.code

There were two different forms of administration for the questionnaires.
IAQ stands for Interviewer Administered Questionnaire.  SAQ means Self-
Administered Questionnaire.

There are two different versions of the data in SPSS and SAS form.  In SPSS 
form, nic123.por contains the data with no missing values identified, while
nic123_2.por contains the data with the missing values specified.  In SAS, 
nic123.ssd01 contains the data with no missing values specified, while
nic123_2.ssd01 contains the data with the missing values specified.  
The data for the codebook can be found at:
     /home/gov/staff/poll96/nic/NIC_data_99/

There were five (5) string variables in the NIC data.  OCCUPAT1 refers 
to the participant's occupation.  There are a large number of occupations, 
too many to be listed here.  HSTPOTH refers to "Hispanic Type Other Specified."
The other Hispanic types include Guatamalan, Chilian, Columbian, Columbian
Puerto Rican, Indian, Spain, and Spanish.  

SAQOTH refers to what other people were present during the 
Self-Administered Questionnaire.  The responses to this question are as 
follows:  7 year old son, adult child, escort, female, one person for 
5 questions, kid playing, mother, wife, small child, son because R is elderly,
children, children under 6, grandchildren, kids, neighbors kept passing, 
young child, part of time, persons, small child then wife, children and 
spouse, brother, two children, don't know, friend dropped in, granddaughter 
and her husband, husband would wander, NY Times reporter, one child, phone, 
probably 8-10 customers, sometimes one kid, sometimes two kids.

The fourth and fifth string variables are Q40_OTHE and Q45_OTHE.  Both
questions ask for the "Other Moderator" (verbatim).  The only valid response 
for this variable is "McNeal."

IAQDATE1:  DATE OF IAQ COMPLETION T1 (Q42 IAQ)
	NOTE:  This variable is unreliable.  A quick look at a frequency of this variable shows that there are many invalid dates.  For example, the "earliest" date listed is "041155" (DDMMYY), which translates to November 4th, 1955.  However, the interviews were conducted from Nov 4th 1995 to Jan 18th, 1996.  (Obviously, no interviews were conducted in 1955!)  
	Another common error in the data we received from NORC is that some dates were obviously recorded in the DDMMYY format, while others were recorded in the MMDDYY format.  Most of these can be "figured out" since we know that only dates from Nov 4th, 1995 to Jan 18th, 1996 are valid responses.  However, the correct date for some observations can not be determined.  Is "111295"  December 11th or November 12th?
	Yet another common problem is that interviewers or those who entered the data in the NORC computer had a hard time remembering that January started the new year.  For example, there are several dates such as "040195."  This should obviously be January 4th, 1996, since no interviews were conducted in January of 1995.
	I (Dennis Plane) tried to sort out these issues to the best of my ability.  My best estimate of the "true" date of the IAQ can be found in the data files "iaqdate.sav" and "iaqdate.ddd01".  Here I make assumptions about what the true dates should be.  For example, I changed the date "041155" to "041195" and the date "040195" to "040196" and the date "122095" to "201295".  For ambiguous dates such as "111295," I stayed with the NORC value assuming the DDMMYY format.
	This became an issue when writing the McCombs chapter where Luskin wanted to treat the response to the Bosnia question different according to the date of the interview.  (See footnote 11 in the McCombs chapter."
